03 / Picture it

The hallway analogy

Imagine trying to move through a hallway of people. You represent a charge carrier; the people represent the lattice. Their movements can interrupt your progress along the hallway.

In the phonon-limited picture, a colder lattice has smaller thermal vibrations and less disruption. Heating strengthens those vibrations, so scattering interrupts directed progress more effectively. Random motion can become faster even while average progress along the hallway becomes slower.

This analogy illustrates disrupted directed motion. Atoms still vibrate at 100 K, and real phonon scattering is a quantum interaction with collective lattice vibrations, rather than literal collisions with larger atoms.

04 / Further considerations

Does mobility always decrease with heating?

Not always. These plots isolate phonon scattering, but charged impurities can also limit mobility. In an impurity-dominated regime, heating can increase mobility because faster carriers can be deflected less strongly by impurity fields. Comparing phonon-limited, impurity-limited, and combined mobility would therefore show competing trends: the combined mobility can rise at low temperature and fall when phonons dominate at higher temperature. Which behavior occurs depends on the material, carrier concentration, and temperature range.
